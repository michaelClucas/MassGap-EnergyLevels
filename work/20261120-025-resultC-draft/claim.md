## Question

Can the Yang-Mills mass gap (pure SU(N), 4D, continuum, infinite volume; Clay formulation: OS/Wightman theory with spectral gap Delta > 0) be explained with the mathematics behind electron energy levels and gaps: bound-state quantization and its lower-bound methods, band theory, interaction-driven gaps (BCS, Mott, Peierls, excitonic), spin-chain spectral-gap methods (AKLT, Knabe, martingale, Haldane), and quantum-chemistry methods?

## Answer (one line)

**Candidate C: obstruction, partial.** Every technique family the program examined proves a gap only where Yang-Mills already has one (strong coupling, fixed lattice spacing with finite volume, or a frustration-free model that is not Yang-Mills); each failure is shown by explicit calculation or counterexample for the route as the program instantiated it, and rows marked argued or open name what would have to change for the route to work. Row 3 (Gribov region / Payne-Weinberger) is open, conditional on the Moncrief/Mondal log-concavity hypothesis, and SDP/bootstrap lower-bound hierarchies were not examined. Draft pending the open checks above.

Target numbers (lattice, continuum-extrapolated by the cited authors, `data/`, observation not proof): 4D SU(3) m(2++)/m(0++) = 1.437(11) [N1], m(0-+)/m(0++) = 1.549(16) [N2], m(0++)/sqrt(sigma) = 3.405(21) [N3]; 4D SU(inf) m(0++)/sqrt(sigma) = 3.072(14) [N4]; 2+1D SU(3) m(0++)/sqrt(sigma) = 4.368(7) [N5].

## What remains open

- **SDP/bootstrap lower-bound hierarchies for a gauge-theory gap** (row 6; leading open direction). Variational 2-RDM (Nakata et al. 2001, Mazziotti 2004), the lattice-YM positivity bootstrap (2203.11360, 2404.16925, 2502.14421) and gap bootstraps (2211.03819, 2510.08427, 2610.18854) are the one published route to rigorous lower bounds that needs neither frustration-freeness nor a truncation floor. No application to a gauge-theory gap was found, and the program did not examine it.
- **Moncrief/Mondal log-concavity of J psi_0^2 on Lambda** (row 3). If it holds (strongly log-concave), a conditional Bakry-Emery bound on the orbit space would be diameter-free, so row 3(d) does not block that variant. Row 3 is open on this hypothesis.
- **Explicit beta-dependence of the 2D Langevin / H_FF gap** (C8). Piazza-Shen give volume uniformity at every beta with a constant that is not explicit (doubly exponential in beta on our reading); our upper bound is 16.5 at the sampled beta. A polynomial-in-beta Poincare constant would be new, but concerns a z = 2 model, not YM.
- **Rigorous 1/N mass generation for 2D CP^{N-1}** (C10). Two searches found only formal 1/N work (confirmed by `029`: none found 2020-2026). It would be the cleanest "gap equation + gauge field + control" stepping stone; it does not reach 4D YM.
- **Certified bounds with exponentially larger truncations, or a better comparison operator** (rows 1, 6, 7). Nothing in principle stops a floor from clearing E1 at weaker coupling on a fixed small lattice; with the norm-bound floor it needs j_max >~ sqrt(N_p)/g (heuristic) and an exponential basis. This extends the finite-lattice table; it does not touch the continuum limit.
- **Comparison with arXiv:2611.11534** (row 1): its one-plaquette gap intervals and hard-cutoff theorem were not compared with [N6]/[N7] and row 1(a); its body could not be read (`029`).
- **Bound-state models at lattice accuracy** (row 8): every model checked, pre- and post-2008, sits at the 5-10% level against ~1% lattice errors. The functional BSE is closest (0 fitted parameters) and would need its 0++*, 2-+, 3++ ratios to move by ~25% and its errors to shrink ~5-10x (`022`). Bottom-up holographic fits (1708.08968, Rinaldi-Vento) were not digitized; the search was arXiv-abstract only.
- **Free-boundary version of Piazza-Shen; d = 3 Hessian constant; no-finite-range FF form of KS** (conjectured, row 2).
- **Boundedness of the lattice Gribov region for all L >= 3, and diam(Lambda) on any lattice** (row 3).

## What the program did NOT examine

- Constructive RG approaches (Balaban's multiscale program) and continuum stochastic quantization; these are not electron-level methods but are the main known routes to the continuum.
- SDP/bootstrap lower-bound hierarchies (v2RDM; lattice-YM positivity bootstrap 2203.11360 / 2404.16925 / 2502.14421; gap bootstrap 2211.03819 / 2510.08427 / 2610.18854). They are the one published route to rigorous lower bounds that needs neither frustration-freeness nor a truncation floor; no application to a gauge-theory gap was found.
- Feynman's bounded-configuration-space heuristic (Nucl. Phys. B 188, 479 (1981)) and the Karabali-Kim-Nair 2+1D gauge-invariant-variable gap m = e^2 N/(2 pi) from the measure (hep-th/9705087; leading order, not rigorous): summarized in `001` and `literature/04`, not analysed.

## Literature check (summary)

This obstruction is mostly a synthesis of known facts plus our explicit calculations. Known before this program, and cited as such in the work folders: strong-coupling lattice gaps (Osterwalder-Seiler; Yarotsky's theorem covers KS with Gauss law, `007`); Bakry-Emery for lattice YM (Shen-Zhu-Zhu; our C6 is their condition in Hamiltonian form); the 2D all-beta LSI (Piazza-Shen 2610.35286); the RK construction (Henley cond-mat/0311345; Castelnovo-Chamon-Mudry-Pujol, Ann. Phys. 318, 316 (2005); Fendley-Fradkin remark cond-mat/0502071), and gap(H_FF) as the ground-state transform of the Langevin generator; a martingale gap proof for a frustration-free deformation of 3+1D Z2 gauge theory (Gorantla-Huang 2409.10612, the closest prior art for row 2) and FF gap criteria with boundary (Lemm-Mozgunov 1801.08915); KS not frustration-free (structural, standard); theta as Bloch momentum (Jackiw-Rebbi, Callan-Dashen-Gross) and multi-branch E(theta) (Witten); Gribov convexity (Zwanziger, Dell'Antonio-Zwanziger) and the open log-concavity hypothesis (Moncrief-Marini-Maitra, Mondal); Coulomb-gauge BCS gluon vacuum (Szczepaniak-Swanson-Ji-Cotanch); Nielsen-Olesen instability; Froehlich-Spencer, Kupiainen, Kopper-Magnen-Rivasseau, Goepfert-Mack, Chatterjee 2602.00436 (3D central-U(1) log-confinement); positivity violation of Gribov propagators; coupled cluster for KS (Schuette, Wichmann et al.); area-law direction (Hastings); RDM/SDP lower bounds and bootstraps (row 6); AKLT, Knabe, Gosset-Mozgunov, and the stronger certified AKLT bound >= 0.34976 (Rai et al., Quantum 10, 2065 (2026)) and improved martingale bounds (Rozman-Molnar-Schuch 2607.19078); the O(3) strong-coupling series (Hamer-Kogut-Susskind 1979) and the O(3) mass gap via TBA (Hasenfratz-Maggiore-Niedermayer, not rigorous); constituent-gluon models (Kaidalov-Simonov, Mathieu-Buisseret-Semay), whose spurious 1-+ is published; functional Bethe-Salpeter glueballs (Huber-Fischer-Sanchis-Alepuz) and 2025 constituent/helicity updates, none within lattice errors (`022`), so no Result B exists in the literature checked. No published result contradicts a row's obstruction (`029`: searches 2020-2026). What is ours: the certified two-sided KS gap intervals on small lattices (no refereed prior certified intervals found; the unrefereed preprints arXiv:2611.11534 (complement and Schur bounds, a certified one-plaquette benchmark, and a hard-cutoff obstruction requiring growth of retained representations) and arXiv:2503.15539 (fixed-cutoff SU(3) gap certificate at strong coupling) overlap with rows 1-2; overlap in numbers is unconfirmed, they were not checked against our numbers), the explicit counterexamples and toy calculations in rows 2-6, the C8 U(1) and SU(2) upper bounds, the side-by-side calibration on the O(3) chain, and the consolidated table. No item here is a new theorem about Yang-Mills; no novelty is claimed for the obstruction as a whole.

## Known weaknesses

- Floating-point certification: ladder, torus and rotor intervals were computed in floating point with a rounding margin 10 n eps ||A||, then rechecked by LDL^T inertia counts with a measured residual (024); none of this is interval arithmetic. Only the single plaquette is proved in exact arithmetic (skeptic 010).
- Lanczos completeness: the inertia-count check (024, `src/inertia.py`, LDL^T with explicit residual and Weyl correction, floating point) verified every C5/C9 lower bound (none failed; a few only after lowering E by <= 1.4e-7) and the rotor rows with L <= 3. Still conditional on Lanczos completeness: rotor rows at L = 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 and AKLT eps_12 (GM >= 0.3026); the Lanczos-free AKLT bound is GM >= 0.3011 (dense n = 10). The Lanczos-dependent eps_12 value is superseded anyway by the published certified bound >= 0.34976 (Rai et al.). 69 further rotor rows at dim > 1500 were not attempted.
- The Gosset-Mozgunov prefactor 5(n^2+n)/(6(n^2-4)) used in `015/spin1.py` was not checked against the paper; N22/N23 values depend on it, positivity of the bound does not.
- Citations: the bibliographic data of Bazley-Fox, Hainzl-Seiringer, FHNS, Savvidy, Nielsen-Olesen, Hasenfratz-Maggiore-Niedermayer, Goepfert-Mack, Aizenman-Holley and Wu now resolve (Crossref, `029`); their theorem content was not re-read. Maehly 1952 still does not resolve. Goepfert-Mack's full text was not read. Most literature content was checked against abstracts only.
- The j_max >~ sqrt(N_p)/g (row 1) and l_max ~ sqrt(N/g) (row 7) scalings are heuristic estimates, not derived bounds.
- Small lattices: all KS numbers are 2+1D SU(2) on <= 4 plaquettes; d = 3 Gribov diameters on L <= 6 with few restarts (lower bounds). Torelon contamination dominates the 2x2 torus.
- Lean coverage is partial: formalized with no sorry are the C4 abstract steps (finite-dimensional, two fibers), the C5 Schur lemma in both the E0 form and the k-eigenvalue count (`schur_count_sorted`; k = 1 is the E1 lower bound used in C5), the easy half of Courant-Fischer (`lean/MassGap/MinMax.lean`, work 026), and the C2 second-order arithmetic. Not formalized: the full Courant-Fischer equality, the direct-integral version of C4, the matrix-element inputs, and every analytic statement.
- Some numbers are recorded, not regenerated: the 2x2 torus j_max = 2 block builds (~14 min and ~5 GB each) and the full rotor-chain certification run (> 30 min) are not rerun by `src/reproduce.py`; their numbers are checked against the committed outputs, and the torus intervals are recomputed from the recorded block bounds (`016/check_c9.py`). See `number_map.csv` (mode `recorded`).
- One transcription error found while drafting: `CLAIMS.md` C5 and the 006 README quote the ladder gap at g^2 = 1 as 3.0620125; `006/results.csv` gives [3.0620132814, 3.0620132822] (and 003 ED 3.062013). This draft uses 3.0620133 [N8].

## Reproduce

From the repo root, one thread:

    OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python tools/pin.py .venv/Scripts/python.exe -m src.reproduce --quick   # 72 s, checks the 29 quick-mode numbers
    OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python tools/pin.py .venv/Scripts/python.exe -m src.reproduce           # full mode, est. 2-2.5 h at 1 thread (README.md)
    cd lean && lake build                                                                      # Lean lemmas (not run by reproduce.py)

Scripts run on copies under `artifacts/<work folder>/`; committed outputs are not touched. The run ends with a pass/fail count over `number_map.csv`. Quick and full mode share `artifacts/`, so two runs must not overlap; `src/reproduce.py` takes the lock `artifacts/.reproduce.lock` and refuses to start while it exists.

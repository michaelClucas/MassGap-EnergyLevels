# 20261120-003: SU(2) Kogut-Susskind exact diagonalization on tiny 2+1D lattices

## Question

What is the gap Delta(g) = E1 - E0 of the SU(2) Kogut-Susskind Hamiltonian, in the gauge-invariant sector, on the smallest nontrivial lattices (single plaquette, 2-plaquette ladder, periodic 2x2), from strong to weak coupling? Does it agree with strong-coupling perturbation theory and with the weak-coupling harmonic limit, and where does the j_max truncation stop being trustworthy?

Setting of every number below: 2+1D Hamiltonian lattice, gauge group SU(2), lattice spacing a = 1 (all energies in lattice units), finite volume (1, 2 or 4 plaquettes), open boundary except the 2x2 torus. Every gap here is a numerical observation at finite volume and finite lattice spacing. None of it is a statement about the continuum.

## Method

### Hamiltonian (normalization)

    H = (g^2/2) sum_links E_l^2 - (1/g^2) sum_plaq Tr(U_p + U_p^dag)
      = (g^2/2) sum_links j_l(j_l+1) - (2/g^2) sum_plaq Tr U_p

Tr is the fundamental (2x2) trace. For SU(2), Tr U is real, so Tr(U + U^dag) = 2 Tr U as a multiplication operator. E_l^2 is the quadratic Casimir on link l (left and right Casimirs coincide), with eigenvalue j(j+1) on the spin-j sector. This is the standard Kogut-Susskind form with a = 1 and no additive constant. The vacuum energy therefore goes to -(4/g^2)(number of plaquettes) plus zero-point terms at weak coupling.

### Basis (spin networks)

- Link Hilbert space L^2(SU(2)) with orthonormal basis |j m n> = sqrt(2j+1) D^j_{mn}(U) (Condon-Shortley Wigner D), truncated at j <= j_max.
- At each vertex the leg indices are contracted with an orthonormal basis of invariant tensors (intertwiners). These are computed numerically, without any 3j/6j tables, as the common null space of the total su(2) generators on the leg indices: row indices (link starts at v) transform with J^T and column indices (link ends at v) with -J. In practice: the weight-0 subspace, then the kernel of the total lowering operator restricted to it. The resulting spin networks are orthonormal and satisfy Gauss's law exactly at every vertex.
- The basis is generated by breadth-first search from the strong-coupling vacuum (all j = 0) under the plaquette operators. This picks out the sector connected to the vacuum. On the torus this is the zero 't Hooft electric-flux sector: winding j = 1/2 strings are gauge invariant but never connected to the vacuum. On the torus all momenta are included, with no momentum or point-group resolution.

### Plaquette matrix elements

Tr U_p multiplies the four loop links by D^{1/2}. The Clebsch-Gordan series

    D^{1/2}_{ab} D^j_{mn} = sum_{J=j+-1/2} C^{J,a+m}_{1/2 a; j m} C^{J,b+n}_{1/2 b; j n} D^J_{a+m,b+n}

puts one CG factor at each end of each link. For links traversed backwards, U^dag_{ab} = (-1)^{b-a} D^{1/2}_{-b,-a}, and the sign splits into one factor per end. The fundamental index summed at each plaquette corner lives at a single vertex, so

    <G'| Tr U_p |G> = prod_{4 links} sqrt((2j+1)/(2J+1)) * prod_{4 corners} <I'_v | T_v I_v>

where T_v applies the two CG maps of the corner's two loop legs, summed over the shared index. All of this is computed numerically in `src/ks_su2.py`.

### Validation (`validate.py`, output in `run.log`/`checks.txt`)

1. CG series identity and conj(D^j_{mn}) = (-1)^{m-n} D^j_{-m,-n}, checked at random group elements for j <= 5/2: max error 8e-16.
2. Gauge invariance: spin-network wavefunctions psi_G(U) are evaluated pointwise by contracting D matrices with intertwiners, then compared before and after a random gauge transformation at every vertex. Max error 5e-15 on all three lattices.
3. **Pointwise matrix-element test.** At random link configurations U, check (Tr U_p psi_G)(U) = sum_G' <G'|Tr U_p|G> psi_G'(U) for 25 random basis states per lattice, every plaquette, with the truncation one step above the states tested so the identity is exact. Max error 9e-15 on plaq1 (j<=2), ladder (j<=3/2), and periodic 2x2 (j<=1). This checks the matrix elements independently of any recoupling convention.
4. W = sum_p Tr U_p is Hermitian to 1e-15 on every basis.
5. Single plaquette: |W| equals the analytic class-function result Tr U chi_j = chi_{j-1/2} + chi_{j+1/2}, i.e. a tridiagonal matrix with unit off-diagonals (deviation 2e-15). The diagonal is 4 j(j+1).
6. Single plaquette, independent solver on the group manifold: class functions psi(theta) with U_p ~ exp(i theta n.sigma) and phi = sin(theta) psi. All four link Laplacians act as the full Laplacian on U_p, giving

       H phi = (g^2/2)(-phi'' - phi) - (4/g^2) cos(theta) phi,   phi(0) = phi(pi) = 0

   (Mathieu-like; proved by direct computation from chi_j = sin((2j+1)theta)/sin theta and C chi_j = j(j+1) chi_j). Solved by Richardson-extrapolated finite differences. It agrees with the spin basis (j_max = 40) on E0 and E1 to <= 1.2e-8 at all ten couplings.
7. Ladder reflection symmetry. All ladder vertices are 2- or 3-valent, so intertwiners are unique and the reflection maps each basis state to +- another one. The signs are fixed along a spanning tree, and then [P, W] = 0, P^2 = 1 and |sign| = 1 are checked on all entries (error <= 6e-15). spec(P+) union spec(P-) equals the full spectrum to 1e-12.

### Runs

g^2 in {0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5, 10}.
- plaq1: j_max from 1/2 to 40, max dim 81.
- ladder2: j_max 1/2 to 8, max dim 1341, split by reflection parity P = +-.
- periodic2x2: j_max 1/2, 1, 3/2, dims 23 / 622 / 9418.

Dense diagonalization for dim <= 1500, otherwise Lanczos (eigsh). One core, total wall time about 4 minutes.

### Strong coupling (checks)

- Leading order (proved, elementary): the lowest excitation raises one plaquette's four links to j = 1/2, costing (g^2/2) * 4 * 3/4 = 3g^2/2.
- Single plaquette, second order (proved: nondegenerate Rayleigh-Schrodinger PT on the tridiagonal matrix):

      Delta = 3g^2/2 + 56/(15 g^6) + O(g^-14)

  At g^2 = 10, 5, 3 this gives 15.003733, 7.529867 and 4.638272. ED gives 15.003733, 7.529791 and 4.635626, so the deviation grows like the next order as expected. PT fails below g^2 of about 2.
- On the 2x2 torus the one-plaquette excitations form a 4-fold multiplet. Their mutual mixing splits them already at order 1/g^6, which lowers Delta below 3g^2/2: 14.86 at g^2 = 10.

### Weak coupling (checks)

- Single plaquette (harmonic limit derived here; status: proved for the leading term, standard semiclassics). Near theta = 0 the equation above is a 3D isotropic oscillator in the s-wave, because class functions are s-waves on S^3 ~ R^3 near the identity. The mass is 1/g^2 and the potential is (2/g^2) theta^2, so omega = 2, independent of g. The first s-wave excitation is 2 omega, giving **Delta -> 4 as g -> 0 in lattice units, not Delta ~ g**. ED: 3.8716, 3.8045 and 3.6636 at g^2 = 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5. (4 - Delta)/g^2 is 0.64, 0.65 and 0.67, consistent with an O(g^2) correction (observation).
- General small lattice, harmonic approximation (conjectured as the g -> 0 limit; derived at leading order). Write U_l = exp(i A_l . sigma) with E_l = -(i/2) d/dA_l. Then H ~ (g^2/8) sum p_l^2 + (2/g^2) sum_p |B_p|^2, where B_p is the signed sum of A_l around plaquette p. The normal frequencies are omega_k = sqrt(lambda_k), with lambda_k the eigenvalues of the plaquette Gram matrix M_{pq} = sum_l c_{pl} c_{ql}. Each mode is a color triplet, and the global SU(2) singlet condition forces at least two quanta.
  - Single plaquette: M = 4, giving omega = 2 and Delta = 4. This matches.
  - Ladder: M = [[4, -1], [-1, 4]], giving omega = sqrt(3) (even) and sqrt(5) (odd). Predictions: Delta(P+) -> 2 sqrt(3) = 3.464 and Delta(P-) -> sqrt(3) + sqrt(5) = 3.968.
  - ED at g^2 = 0.2: 3.4005 and 3.847, with slopes (prediction - ED)/g^2 of 0.32 and 0.6, consistent with O(g^2) corrections. This is a nontrivial check of the recoupling signs: a sign error would not reproduce the sqrt(3)/sqrt(5) normal-mode structure.
- So on open tiny lattices the gap in lattice units does **not** shrink at weak coupling. It approaches a g-independent free-field value set by the lattice geometry, i.e. by lattice momenta of order 1/a. That is the lattice spacing cutoff, not a mass.
- 2x2 torus: the weak-coupling regime is not reached. j_max = 3/2 is converged only for g^2 >= 3 (to 1e-2), and below g^2 of about 2 the gap changes by O(1) per j_max step. The dip at g^2 ~ 1 in the plot is a truncation artifact until shown otherwise. On a torus the flat directions (constant commuting holonomies, "torons") have no magnetic restoring force. The expected small-volume behavior is a gap from electric energy on that moduli space, scaling like g^2 in lattice units at weak coupling. Status: cited from memory, references not yet checked by the literature scout (Luscher, Nucl. Phys. B219 (1983) 233; Koller and van Baal, small/intermediate-volume SU(2) spectroscopy, late 1980s). This is not checked here.

### What the continuum would require (not tested here)

In 2+1D, g^2 has mass dimension 1: g^2_lattice = g_3^2 a. A continuum glueball mass m implies a lattice-units gap a m = (m/g_3^2) g^2_lattice -> 0 linearly in g^2 (cited: standard dimensional analysis; lattice values of m/g_3^2 are in the Teper/Athenodorou-Teper SU(N) 2+1D papers, which `data/` should carry). Seeing that requires L >> 1/(m a), i.e. many plaquettes, with j_max converged at g^2 <~ 1. On the lattices here the weak-coupling gap is dominated by finite size and the cutoff (open boundary) or by torons (torus), so these numbers say nothing about the continuum glueball mass.

## Verdict

- The gauge-invariant ED machinery works and is validated: pointwise wavefunction identities, gauge invariance, an independent group-manifold solver, strong-coupling PT, and weak-coupling normal modes all agree. It is reusable from `src/ks_su2.py` for any 2D lattice given as vertices, links and plaquette loops.
- Numerical observation, finite volume and finite a: Delta > 0 at every coupling sampled on all three lattices. For one plaquette and the ladder it is smooth, with a minimum near g^2 ~ 1.5 (3.117 and 2.998), interpolating between 3g^2/2 and the harmonic values 4 and 2 sqrt(3). That a gap exists on any finite lattice at fixed g is expected and not informative. The Hamiltonian is a Schrodinger operator on a compact group manifold, so its spectrum is discrete and its ground state is unique by positivity improvement of the heat semigroup (cited: Reed-Simon IV, Sec. XIII.12, positivity-improving semigroups; the ground state is then gauge invariant because SU(2) has no nontrivial one-dimensional characters).
- The electron-level analogy holds literally here: the single plaquette is a particle on S^3 in a cos(theta) potential, a Mathieu-type bound-state problem. The strong-coupling gap is a "crystal-field" Casimir gap, and the weak-coupling gap is the oscillator gap of the potential well. Neither says anything about the continuum gap.

## Open issues

1. The 2x2 torus needs j_max >= 2 to reach g^2 ~ 1 (estimated dim ~1e5). That needs momentum and point-group block diagonalization, which requires tracking intertwiner-basis rotations under translations because 4-valent vertices have multi-dimensional intertwiner spaces. The basis build (pure Python BFS) is also the current bottleneck: about 2 min for dim 9418.
2. No momentum or J^PC labels on the torus yet. The torus E1 at strong coupling is a member of a one-plaquette multiplet, not necessarily a zero-momentum state. Delta there is "lowest excitation in the zero-flux sector, all momenta".
3. The toron-regime prediction Delta ~ g^2 on the torus is not tested.
4. Larger volumes (3x3, ladders of length > 2) are needed for any finite-size scaling. Exact diagonalization with this basis scales as roughly (2 j_max + 1)^(number of links), so the next step is DMRG/tensor networks or a smarter (dual/loop) basis.
5. Strong-coupling PT for the ladder and torus beyond leading order was not derived analytically. Only the single-plaquette O(1/g^6) term was.
